{\Large\bfseries Speaker--microphone bench verification}\\[-1pt]
{\small SONIC Auditory Benchmarking \hfill 23 October 2026}

\textbf{Established.} Simultaneous speaker playback and microphone recording work.
Two timing runs established an approximately \textbf{38.3\,ms application roundtrip}
in the tested program and wired configuration.

\textbf{Setup.} Computer/Core Audio at 96\,kHz $\to$ Audient iD14 $\to$
Edifier speaker $\to$ Earthworks M50 $\to$ Audient input 1 $\to$ program.
The operator reported 10\,cm speaker--microphone separation; it was not remeasured.

\textbf{SONIC sounds.} We played and recorded one reconstructed Sheep E block using
the paper's exact 14-frequency alphabet (kHz):
\[
\{2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 9,\ 10.5,\ 17,\ 18.2,\ 19.5,\ 20.9,\ 22.4,\ 24,\ 25.8,\ 27\}.
\]
The block contains 8,400 consecutive 10\,ms zero-phase sine pips, with 2\,ms
ramps at each end. The original tone order and ramp shape were unavailable:
our seeded order and raised-cosine ramps are declared reconstruction choices.
Output was 96\,kHz; the paper used 192\,kHz.

\textbf{Timing result.} Forty nonperiodic 100\,Hz--20\,kHz sweeps were matched
from the exact output array to microphone input 1, across two separately started
full-duplex streams.

All probes passed signal and transport checks; there was no clipping or callback
warning. Run medians differed by 0.125\,ms.
Direct delay estimates from the SONIC sequence remained cycle-ambiguous;
\textbf{38.3\,ms is the separate sweep-based result}.

\textbf{Scope.} This establishes short-run repeatability, including playback/recording
buffers, conversion and the acoustic chain. It does not establish absolute accuracy,
isolated speaker delay, calibrated SPL, ultrasonic coverage, or another program's latency.
