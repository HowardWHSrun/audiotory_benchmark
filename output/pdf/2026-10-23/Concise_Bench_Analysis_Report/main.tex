{\LARGE\bfseries\color{navy} SONIC bench test: brief results}\par
{\small 23 October 2026 \hfill Analysis of existing recordings}

\textbf{Setup.} Computer $\to$ Audient iD14 $\to$ Edifier speaker $\to$ Earthworks M50 $\to$ Audient microphone input 1. Microphone separation was approximately 10\,cm (operator reported). Four saved 96\,kHz recordings were analyzed; no new test was performed.

\textbf{Timing.} All 40 timing sweeps were accepted across two stream starts.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{0.94\linewidth}{@{}Xr@{}}
\toprule
Pooled median application roundtrip & \textbf{38.307\,ms}\\
Observed range & 38.240--38.375\,ms\\
Individual run medians & 38.250 / 38.375\,ms\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
This demonstrates short-run repeatability for the tested program and setup. The delay includes computer/interface buffering and the acoustic chain; it does not establish calibrated sub-millisecond accuracy or isolated speaker delay.

\textbf{SONIC sounds.} Two 84-second blocks used a local reconstruction of the paper's 14 Sheep E frequencies, from 2 to 27\,kHz, with 10\,ms pips and 2\,ms ramps: 16,800 pips total. The original SONIC audio files were not used.

\textbf{Frequency results.} Both microphone recordings contain all 14 commanded frequency components, but their levels are uneven. Relative to 2\,kHz, the 17--19.5\,kHz groups were approximately 15--22\,dB lower; 20.9--25.8\,kHz were 30--40\,dB lower. These are full-chain relative recordings. Calibrated SPL, speaker-only response and distortion remain unmeasured.

\textbf{Next.} Fix and document the geometry, apply microphone/SPL calibration, test separated tones, and validate timing against an independent electrical reference.
