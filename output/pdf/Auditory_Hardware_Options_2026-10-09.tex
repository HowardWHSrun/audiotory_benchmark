\textbf{Working totals:} \money{370.18} + \money{80} shipping/tax reserve = \textbf{\money{450.18}}. With our own calibrator: \money{589.18} + \money{100} reserve = \textbf{\money{689.18}}. A recorder-specific cable/adapter, if needed, is additional.

\heading{What could be misaligned?}
\textbf{Start delay.} Buffering alone can add several milliseconds. One 256-sample buffer at 48 kHz lasts \textbf{5.3 ms}; two last 10.7 ms. Converter, speaker and air delays are additional. This is an example, not measured UMC latency. Air adds about \textbf{3 ms per metre} in either setup.

\textbf{Trial variation.} How much the delay changes between trials is \textbf{unmeasured}. Preload the sequence and use wired audio. The moment the program calls ``play'' is not the sound's arrival time.

\textbf{Clock drift.} The interface-to-recorder mismatch is \textbf{unknown}. For scale, a hypothetical \textbf{100 ppm relative mismatch} means \textbf{6 ms per minute, or 0.36 seconds per hour}, if uncorrected. This is an illustration, not a prediction for this hardware.

\heading{How we align it}
Generate a reference alongside each tone on the spare output and record it with the brain data. Measure its offset to sound arrival with the microphone. Recording the mic only on the USB interface still leaves two clocks to align. Audio output is not TTL; recorder inputs determine the adapter. A compatible neural-recorder mic input would give a more direct acoustic reference.

\heading{What precision does it provide?}
\textbf{Sound range:} the paper tested \textbf{1.4--32 kHz}. MF1's stated free-field response is 1--65 kHz, raw $\pm$13 dB. The paper used approximately 80 dB at the ear; it gives no calibrated $\pm$dB uncertainty.

\textbf{Timing:} nominal 192 kSa/s means \textbf{5.21 $\mu$s sample spacing}. TM2500C specifies PTP below 1 $\mu$s \emph{plus network jitter}. Neither number establishes sound-to-neural alignment. The paper reports no quantified acoustic jitter or long-session drift.

\textbf{Drift:} NI's free-running 50 ppm figure equals \textbf{3 ms/min or 180 ms/hour versus ideal time}. This is not SONIC's drift after locking to GPS. Our recorder needs a compatible reference or repeated recorded markers; its relative drift is unknown. Free-running clock-only tone error is $\pm$0.5 Hz at 10 kHz; GPS locking changes that reference.

\heading{What precision does it provide?}
\textbf{Sound range:} ES1 is specified for \textbf{4--110 kHz}, raw $\pm$11 dB. It misses SONIC's 1.4--4 kHz tones. The PXI-4461's stated output flatness extends to 92.1 kHz at its maximum rate. At-ear $\pm$dB accuracy is unknown; electrical flatness is not acoustic accuracy.

\textbf{Timing:} at 204.8 kSa/s, spacing is \textbf{4.88 $\mu$s}. The DAC filter adds \textbf{156.25 $\mu$s} at that rate; speaker and air delay are additional. The slides give no measured sound-to-neural jitter.

\textbf{Drift:} the 4461's internal $\pm$20 ppm specification corresponds to \textbf{1.2 ms/min or 72 ms/hour versus ideal time}, before aging/calibration uncertainty. Clock-only frequency error is $\pm$0.2 Hz at 10 kHz. Shared-rack clock settings and the recorder clock still determine relative drift; the slides do not establish either.

\heading{What precision should we expect?}
\textbf{Sound:} MF2's free-field band is \textbf{1--80 kHz}; M50 covers to 50 kHz. This covers SONIC's band. R8090's \textbf{$\pm$0.5 dB applies at 1 kHz}; whole-band at-ear accuracy remains unmeasured.

\textbf{Timing and drift:} proposed actual 200 kSa/s gives \textbf{5 $\mu$s spacing}, not 5 $\mu$s alignment. Free-running NI 50 ppm means \textbf{180 ms/hour versus ideal time}; clock-only error is $\pm$0.5 Hz at 10 kHz. Record repeated markers and microphone arrivals to estimate offset/drift. Corrected alignment is unmeasured; \textbf{$\leq$1 ms is a proposed acceptance goal}.

\textbf{Budget decision:} use the basic setup for selected audible tones; choose this version for the full SONIC band. No fixed upper budget is defensible until TDT quotes and recorder wiring are resolved. Arduino/FPGA can provide buffered playback/markers, but still needs suitable analog output, amplification, calibration and clock alignment; it is not yet a verified cheaper full-band alternative.
