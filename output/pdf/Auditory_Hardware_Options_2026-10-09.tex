\titleline{Frequency and compatibility limits}{The usable band belongs to the complete chain}

\topic{The inexpensive audible basket}
The specified JBL/EMM-6 bands extend to about 20 kHz. Select tones within the measured usable band and verify their level and distortion at the intended position. A 192 kHz sampling setting is not evidence that this speaker/microphone can deliver or measure ultrasound accurately.

\topic{The ES1 needs a different kind of driver}
\src{https://www.tdt.com/docs/hardware/ec1-es1-electrostatic-speaker/}{TDT ES1} is a free-field electrostatic speaker specified at \textbf{4--110 kHz, $\pm$11 dB}. The related EC1 is a coupler/tubing option, not the same free-field arrangement. TDT cautions that signals below 4 kHz cause substantial harmonic distortion and can degrade these speakers. Neither is a default low-frequency sheep speaker.

A computer or ordinary USB interface cannot directly drive ES1. The pictured chain needs \src{https://www.tdt.com/docs/hardware/ed1-electrostatic-speaker-driver/}{ED1} and compatible zBus power, plus the matching speaker cable. Some integrated TDT systems, including \src{https://www.tdt.com/docs/hardware/rz6-multi-i-o-processor/}{RZ6}, provide electrostatic drive themselves. Do not substitute a conventional amplifier or arbitrary speaker cable. Cable length affects response.

\topic{The slide's audio card limits the top frequency}
\src{https://docs-be.ni.com/bundle/ni-4461-4462-specs/raw/resource/enus/373770k.pdf}{PXI-4461} provides two analog output channels at up to \textbf{204.8 kSa/s}. At that rate, NI specifies flat output through \textbf{92.1 kHz}; the Nyquist boundary is 102.4 kHz. The pictured source therefore does not cover the ES1's entire 110 kHz specification. USB-6361 has a higher-rate waveform source, but its complete analog chain still needs measurement.

\topic{Used PXI hardware requires exact-model checks}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{PXI-4461 version:} NI sold standard PXI and hybrid-compatible versions under the same name. Only the appropriate hybrid variant fits the PXIe-1082's hybrid peripheral slots. The listed cheap card's rear connector/version is unconfirmed. \src{https://knowledge.ni.com/KnowledgeArticleDetails?id=kA00Z000000P8eUSAS}{NI variant explanation}; \src{https://docs-be.ni.com/bundle/pxie-1082-seri/raw/resource/enus/372752c.pdf}{chassis manual}.
\item \textbf{Controller:} use PXIe-8360 with PCIe-8361 and the compatible MXI cable. PXI-8360 is a different controller-slot architecture. \src{https://docs-be.ni.com/bundle/373259d/raw/resource/enus/373259d.pdf}{NI connection guide}.
\item \textbf{Breakout:} one SHC68-68-EPM/BNC-2090A set exposes one bank. A second is needed only if the final channel map uses both. Confirm pinouts and available channels. \src{https://www.ni.com/en/support/documentation/cable-accessory-guide/daq-multifunction-i-o-cable-accessory-compatibility/main-page---daq-multifunction-i-o-cable-and-accessory-compatibil/63xx-models.html}{NI cable/accessory guide}.
\item \textbf{Computer/software:} check OS/driver support and a free desktop PCIe slot. An existing laptop does not directly accept PCIe-8361. NI-DAQmx supports text-language programming; a LabVIEW purchase is not inherently required. \src{https://knowledge.ni.com/KnowledgeArticleDetails?id=kA00Z000000kFD7SAM&l=en-US}{NI software options}.
\end{itemize}

\topic{The full SONIC band needs another speaker choice}
SONIC's initial alphabet spans approximately \textbf{1.4--32 kHz}; its refined set extends through 27 kHz. The 20 kHz audible basket omits the high end; ES1 omits the low end. SONIC used \textbf{USB-6361, SA1 and MF1}, not the slide's ES1 chain. For the full paper range, retain the \src{https://www.tdt.com/docs/hardware/mf2-multi-field-magnetic-speakers/}{MF2/SA1 candidate} or another measured compatible route, with a microphone suitable below 2 kHz. See \src{https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.09.30.679683}{SONIC bioRxiv v1}, Methods 6.1--6.2, p.11.

\titleline{Calibration, timing and our next decisions}{What turns playback into a useful neural experiment}

\topic{Measure actual sound, not only the command}
A software playback timestamp and an audio interface's internal digital loopback do not measure DAC output, speaker response or sound arrival at the ear. The computer, acoustic interface and neural recorder may also have independent clocks. Even modules in one rack need an explicit synchronization plan.

An economical candidate records the analog EMM-6 and an electrical playback monitor through the same audio interface. Reserve an independent physical output for a waveform/reference accepted by the neural recorder's auxiliary analog input, or use a properly conditioned marker for a compatible digital input. \textbf{Audio line output is not TTL.} The recorder model determines allowable voltage, impedance, polarity, connector and grounding.

Record repeated shared markers or an appropriate analog reference, then measure acoustic onset and clock drift. A USB microphone such as UMIK-1 is convenient for calibration but does not automatically share the playback or neural clock. \src{https://psychtoolbox.org/docs/PsychPortAudio-Start}{Psychtoolbox playback timing}; \src{https://psychtoolbox.org/docs/AudioFeedbackLatencyTest}{physical latency test}; \src{https://open-ephys.github.io/gui-docs/Tutorials/Data-Synchronization.html}{Open Ephys synchronization}; \src{https://mail.roomeqwizard.com/help/help_en-GB/html/makingmeasurements.html}{REW clock mismatch}.

\topic{Establish sound levels for the full gain chain}
The EMM-6's individual frequency-response file does not alone establish absolute sound pressure level after microphone-preamp gain changes. Use a suitable SPL reference and keep gains fixed. Measure each chosen tone at the intended ear position. A one-frequency reference does not establish broadband ultrasonic accuracy. \src{https://mail.roomeqwizard.com/help/help_en-GB/html/calsoundcard.html}{REW calibration guidance}.

Disable microphone input monitoring to avoid feedback. Disable automatic gain, noise suppression and sound enhancement. Save the measured response, gain, distance/orientation, background sound, sequence, configuration and timing checks with the recordings.

\topic{What we need to decide}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Choose the frequency range.} Selected audible tones can justify the \cash{1,500} first-setup budget. Full SONIC or rodent ultrasound requires a wider-band speaker and measurement chain.
\item \textbf{Identify the lab recorder.} Confirm its exact probe/headstage interface, spare analog/digital inputs, clock, supported signal levels and exported timestamps.
\item \textbf{Inventory reusable equipment.} Check the computer, wired speakers, microphones, calibrated references, DAQs, TDT power/driver/cables and fixed mounts before buying duplicates.
\item \textbf{Verify the selected chain on the bench.} Check per-tone SPL and distortion, repeatable geometry, physical acoustic onset, timing offset and drift. A price list does not establish these results.
\end{enumerate}

\topic{Rice scope and the paper remain separate}
Rice's primary measurements are planned in \textbf{sedated sheep}; SONIC recorded \textbf{awake, alert sheep}. Hardware feasibility does not establish response windows, decoding accuracy or achieved information-transfer rate under sedation. Lan does not require a soundproof box, behavioral rig or sound isolation; background sound, calibration and geometry still matter. The browser walkthrough is an educational simulation, not hardware control or an animal-ready protocol.
