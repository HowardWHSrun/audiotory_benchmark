\heading{Timing and sound calibration}
\begin{points}
\item \textbf{Actual alignment and drift: unmeasured.} Computer buffering adds delay; one 256-sample buffer at 48,000 samples per second is 5.3 milliseconds. This is an illustration, not a measurement of this interface.
\item Record repeated timing references with the neural signals and use the microphone near the ear to measure sound arrival. Air adds about three milliseconds per metre; the speaker and converters add further delay.
\item Check the sound level of each tone at the ear. Recording the microphone only on the sound interface still requires alignment with the neural timeline.
\end{points}

\heading{Budget and remaining items}
\begin{points}
\item \textbf{Supplier quotes required:} MF1-M speaker, SA1 amplifier and ZB1PS powered enclosure. Confirm speaker kit contents and included power/cable accessories.
\item \textbf{Additional:} microphone connection, recorder timing connection, network wiring, delivery/tax and labor. The time reference is currently backordered.
\end{points}
\heading{Timing and sound calibration}
\begin{points}
\item \textbf{Paper result: 56 milliseconds of neural-processing delay.} This covers neural measurement, filtering and collecting data for decoding. It excludes sensory processing and computer calculation time; it is not a sound-to-neural timestamp error.
\item \textbf{Alignment and drift:} the paper does not quantify remaining sound-to-neural timing error or long-session drift. Its time reference specifies below one microsecond plus network variation; this is not sound-arrival accuracy.
\item Buying these sound parts does not reproduce the paper's neural-processing delay. Test our recorder alignment and at-ear sound level separately.
\end{points}
