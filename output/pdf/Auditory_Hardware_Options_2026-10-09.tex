\heading{Timing}
\begin{points}
\item We have not measured this setup's timing error or drift. For scale, a 256-sample buffer at 48,000 samples per second lasts 5.3 milliseconds; that is a buffer example, not a measured interface delay.
\item Measure sound arrival and each tone's level at the ear. Sound travel adds about three milliseconds per metre, on top of speaker and converter delays.
\item Record repeated timing markers with the neural data to measure accumulating mismatch. A microphone recorded through the sound interface still needs to be aligned with the neural recording.
\end{points}

\heading{Timing}
\begin{points}
\item SONIC reports 56 milliseconds for neural measurement, filtering and collecting data for the decoder. This excludes sensory processing and computer calculation time.
\item The 56 milliseconds is not sound-to-neural timestamp error, and we cannot transfer that result to our recorder by buying the sound hardware.
\item The paper does not quantify remaining alignment error or long-session drift. The time reference specifies below one microsecond plus network variation; that does not describe sound arrival at the ear.
\end{points}

\heading{Timing and frequency range}
\begin{points}
\item The ES1 covers 4--110 kilohertz, so it misses the paper's 1.4--4 kilohertz tones. The audio board's flat output extends to 92.1 kilohertz at 204,800 samples per second.
\item At that rate, the output filter adds a fixed 0.15625 milliseconds. Speaker and air delays are additional; this is not the system's timing accuracy.
\item We have not measured alignment, drift or sound-level accuracy at the ear. The boards' clock settings, recorder connections and acoustic calibration need to be checked together.
\end{points}

\heading{Timing and calibration}
\begin{points}
\item The speaker is nominally rated for 1--80 kilohertz and the microphone to 50 kilohertz. The calibrator's 0.5-decibel specification applies at one kilohertz only; it does not establish accuracy across all our tones.
\item One possible test criterion is to align timestamps of sound arrival with the neural recording within one millisecond after correction. This has not been demonstrated and measures something different from SONIC's processing delay.
\item Measure repeated markers, sound arrival and converter channel offsets. The setup has not been built or validated; its remaining alignment error and drift are unknown.
\end{points}
