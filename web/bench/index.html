How are you connected?

Measures the whole playback-to-microphone roundtrip: browser buffers, converters, speaker, air travel, and microphone. It does not isolate acoustic travel time.

Timing options & interpretation

The 100 ms, 100 Hz–20 kHz chirp is a timing probe, separate from SONIC tone pips. Distance is recorded in the export; travel time is not subtracted.

The electrical method compares the mic with a simultaneous recorded line return. Shared playback buffering cancels in that comparison; speaker, air, microphone response, and analog channel differences remain. The browser must expose both independent inputs.

Browser resampling, driver buffering and unreported microphone processing can affect timing. Unknown processing, weak or ambiguous signal, clipping, and missing reference channels prevent a usable result. A browser screening result is not independently calibrated timing accuracy or a correction for another application’s timestamps.

Bench limits

Connect opens microphone access; a delay run retains the input recording in this tab. Disconnect releases the microphone. Keep input monitoring off to avoid feedback. Delay screening does not calibrate acoustic SPL, microphone sensitivity, frequency response, or neural synchronization. A 27 kHz digital tone does not establish 27 kHz acoustic delivery. The paper used awake, alert sheep; Rice’s planned sedated-sheep study is an unverified adaptation. This is a bench tool, not an animal-ready protocol.
