What we testedSetup, position and stimulus

An Audient iD14 at 96 kHz played 14 reconstructed SONIC Sheep E frequencies through the connected speaker. An Earthworks M50 recorded input 1. The speaker model and intended sheep-ear geometry were not independently confirmed.

Howard reported the microphone about 10 cm from the speaker and unchanged gains. During the calibration attempt he moved the microphone sideways twice, about 5 mm each time. The final correction applies only to that resulting position and the same routing, orientation and gain settings. Recheck after any change.

Our local reconstruction uses 10 ms pips with 2 ms raised-cosine end ramps. The original SONIC waveform is unavailable. Test output was at or below a −36 dBFS digital peak; that number is not sound pressure in the room.

How the results were checkedSeparated pips and back-to-back playback

We measured pips separated by 300 ms, then reduced stronger frequencies without boosting the weakest. After a further attenuation-only adjustment, all 14 groups passed the local screens in one isolated-pip run; their central-component medians spanned 1.60 dB. Two subsequent seven-second previews of 700 consecutive pips spanned 1.13 dB and 0.77 dB across frequency-group medians. These are single-configuration bench checks, not per-pip equality.

With the earlier correction profile, an isolated-pip repeat spanned 1.70 dB; one 9 kHz trial did not pass in the preceding run. Two earlier seven-second previews of 700 consecutive, balanced pips had 14 group medians spanning 1.85 dB and 1.91 dB after excluding nearby potentially interfering tones. Each group had 10–43 qualifying pips; these are group medians, not a guarantee for each sound. For example, the first run’s 24 kHz group had 10 qualifying pips and its fifth percentile was 3.1 dB below its median.

Microphone and SPL referenceWhat the calibration does—and does not—establish

Howard confirmed a REED R8090 calibrator set to 114 dB, with the M50 fitted using its adapter and microphone gain unchanged. The recorded reference was stable near 1 kHz and not digitally clipped. Its output and fit were not independently verified.

Using that reference and assuming a flat microphone response and unchanged gain, the latest consecutive pips’ central-frequency components correspond to roughly 41–42 dB equivalent-sine levels. These are conditional estimates, not verified whole-pip RMS SPL or peak-equivalent SPL. Broadband background was substantial in earlier full 10 ms windows.

The M50’s printed individual on-axis response chart appears approximately flat over the test band, but the electronic calibration file and a full-band uncertainty assessment have not been applied. The 1 kHz reference alone does not calibrate the entire 2–27 kHz path. A provisional 47–48 dB SPL estimate applies to an earlier corrected long-tone run under stated assumptions; it is not a measured level for the final pips.

The 96 kHz recording cannot independently qualify all ultrasonic harmonics. At the intended ear geometry, establish absolute pip level and a defined level convention, check distortion and background, and repeat the position and playback tests before experimental use.

Delay is a separate measurementWhat the earlier 38 ms result includes

Two separately opened native streams produced median playback-to-microphone roundtrip estimates of 38.250 and 38.375 ms for a timing sweep. Those include the computer and interface buffers, converters, speaker, air and microphone path. They are not independently calibrated timing accuracy, neural latency, or a measurement of SONIC’s intrinsic delay.
