THE PART THAT IS EASY TO MISS

Fast tones. Shared neural context.

At 10-ms tone spacing, adjacent 50-ms observation windows share 40 ms of data. Each tone still receives its own prediction, so a long window does not require slower output.

WHY THESE DIFFER

Accuracy is only part of the story.

More possible tones make exact answers harder, but each answer can carry more information. Nearby-frequency errors can still preserve useful information.

THE METRIC

Information per tone, divided by time.

The decoder's predictions produce a confusion matrix. Mutual information measures how much those predictions reduce uncertainty about the true tone; the authors correct its finite-sample bias.

ITR = corrected mutual information ÷ tone duration

At 100 tones/s, the reported means represent approximately 2.02 informative bits per tone for Sheep C and 2.30 for Sheep E.

WHAT TO TAKE AWAY

A strong demonstration, with a defined scope.

Demonstrated here

  • Rapid auditory responses contain decodable tone information.
  • The complete implanted Connexus system supports high information throughput in this task.
  • Useful information remains in brief neural observation windows.

Still untested here

  • Rice's sedated preparation and its actual recording system.
  • Human speech or motor-control performance at these rates.
  • Channel-count scaling, long-term decoding stability, and device comparisons under the same benchmark.

The two sheep were selected for the largest auditory-cortex coverage in a larger cohort (~334 and ~341 of 421 electrodes). Session repetition does not establish a permanently stable decoder. The 2025 source is a preprint.
